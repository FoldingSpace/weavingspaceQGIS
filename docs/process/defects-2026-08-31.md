# Defects of 2026-08-31: round five, and the day the campaign closed

Twenty defects found, repaired, guarded by a registered test each, and
every guard watched failing before it was counted. This ledger exists
because the other places cannot hold it: ROADMAP.md carries only what
is outstanding, so a defect fixed WITH a guard drops out of it;
`dev/state-of-play.md` is gitignored and rewritten every session; and a
conversation ends.

The OWES column is the one to keep honest. `--` would mean the fix
rests on its hunt's reproduction alone, which is not what this project
calls fixed. There are none: every row below carries a registered test,
and every row but two carries a catalogue entry proved `caught`. The
two exceptions say why at the row.

## How the day ran

The maintainer asked for every procedural document to be re-read before

Then round five: EIGHT hunts at once, each in its own worktree under

0 disagreements and 0 harness faults of its own, including the five
boundary crossings that went red the first time it ever ran.

TWO DEFECTS WERE FOUND BY TWO HUNTS INDEPENDENTLY, from different
directions, which is the strongest confirmation this method produces
because neither can be an artefact of the other's fixture.

THREE OF THE TWENTY WERE IN THE GATES rather than in the plugin. A
defect in the plugin draws a wrong map; a defect in a gate produces a
number everybody believes.

## The prose rows, and why they have no catalogue entry

Rows 9, 10 and 11 are sentences rather than code. A test asserting the
words would pin the WORDING rather than the truth and would fail the
next time somebody rewrote the sentence correctly -- which this project
has already paid for twice, once this very day (see below). What guards
them is the prose hunt that found them, and the maintainer's review,
which is the act that puts them in front of a person.

Row 9 has one mechanical guard: `test_the_documents_numbers_match_the_
code` reads the ceiling out of the catalogue and compares it with the
guide. It reads the GUIDE ONLY, which is exactly why the changelog kept
the wrong number after the guide was corrected. Widening it to the
changelog is a candidate for the next round and is recorded in
ROADMAP.md rather than done in a hurry.

## Upstream moved under us, and took one of our findings with it

Checked at the maintainer's asking, after the candidate had started:
upstream is TWELVE COMMITS ahead of the vendored `bf1bbbf`, at
`6190917`, with `topology.py` (+179/-207) and `_tiling_geometries.py`
(+44/-67) both changed. **The version string did not move** -- it is
`0.0.7.89` at both ends, which is precisely the case the stamp records
a commit for.

One of those commits, `b3650e0`, is *"fixed bug in zigzag edges code
where it was doubling up tiling vertices and creating invalid
polygons"*, and its diff is two lines: the endpoints were added twice,
once as `edge.vertices[0]`/`[-1]` and again from `ls.coords`, which
includes them. That is exactly what this project measured on
2026-08-30 -- six coincident point pairs among thirty-seven points,
twelve of twenty tiles invalid on chavey K -- and the "conversation to
have" in ROADMAP.md is answered at the source rather than in every
consumer.

The re-vendor is 0.24.5's, by the maintainer's decision, and it wants
its own round: twelve commits touching `topology.py` heavily is not a
licence-header bump, and the tab, the five manipulations, the edit
shelf and the topology matrix all stand on that module.

## AND A GATE THAT HAD STOPPED SEEING, found by planting a fault

A reference to a script that does not exist, appended to CLAUDE.md,
PASSED `test_every_documented_command_still_exists`.

THE CAUSE WAS THIS DAY'S OWN LESSON. The entry written that morning to
describe the fenced-block fault opened with a dash and three literal
backticks; `_prose_outside_fences` toggles only on a line that STARTS
with the fence, so three unpaired backticks stayed in the prose and
every span below them paired with its wrong neighbour -- at the END of
the file, which is where this project appends every lesson.

MEASURED BOTH WAYS: with the odd count the plant passes and the gate
reports 160 quotations, 38 scripts, 19 long flags -- unchanged though
sixteen spans had just been added, which is the uniform-verdict tell
arriving as a count that would not move. Rewritten to describe the
delimiter rather than quote it: the plant fails by name and the gate
reports 163, 39, 20. Two of the six documents it reads were odd.

The documents are mended; the GATE still pairs across a whole document,
and that repair is under 0.24.5 with the control it needs.
