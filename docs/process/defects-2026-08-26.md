# The defects of 2026-08-26: a candidate that went red, and eight hunts aimed at its repairs

Written the day they were found, per the rule that a day passing
roughly ten defects gets a ledger and that the OWES column is the
record rather than any sentence above it.

THE DAY HAS THREE PARTS. The morning's candidate failed three tests,
on every platform, and NOT ONE was a product defect: four tests were
asserting a contract the maintainer retired on 2026-08-25, an oracle
was judging from before a legitimate replacement, and a test was
holding a widget across a debounce that had been shortened hours
earlier. The evening's FIRST round of eight hunts, aimed at the seven
fixes made that morning, found four real defects, three of them
confirmed by hunts that could not see each other. Its SECOND round of
eight, aimed an hour later at those repairs, found five more -- and
four of those eight hunts landed on a single line written in that
hour.

ELEVEN DEFECTS AND FOUR TEST FAULTS IN ONE DAY, sixteen rows in all
counting the ruling and the false claim. Nine of the eleven are in
code written on 2026-08-25 or 2026-08-26, and SEVEN are inside repairs
made the same day -- three of them inside repairs made in the same
hour. This project's own history: eleven of fourteen within hours on
08-16, four of five on 08-20, nine of eleven on 08-25. The claim that
a round aimed at the last round's repairs always finds something now
stands at SIX ROUNDS FOR SIX.

AND THE SUITE COULD NOT HAVE FOUND ANY OF THEM. The candidate they
were found in had passed 574 tests, the visual gallery on three QGIS
versions, the colourspace comparison against a reference renderer
that had just moved by thirty upstream versions, and six CI jobs. A
gate measures whether known behaviour still holds.

### What round two says, beyond the defects

THREE OF THE FIVE ARE IN REPAIRS MADE THAT HOUR. Rows 11 to 14 are all
inside row 1's and row 2's fixes. The claim in this project's record --
that a round aimed at the last round's repairs finds something every
time -- now stands at SIX ROUNDS FOR SIX.

AND THE TWO THAT ARE NOT are the same shape as each other and as row
16's cause: ONE FACT IN TWO STORES, mended in one. The group's record
and the file's record; the record and the widget. A repair that gives
one store a new write should enumerate every store that already held
that fact, which is now written into docs/TESTING.md.

FOUR HUNTS ON ONE LINE, and none of them could see the others. The
line was present and in the wrong place, which is why no reading of
"is the call there?" would have found it -- and why the entry aimed at
its POSITION could not be made to catch on three separate fixtures,
adoption covering the late recovery by accident through the layer
stamps. That entry is aimed at the call's ABSENCE instead, and the
order is recorded as guarded by these reproductions rather than by the
catalogue. A cover that is accidental is a countdown, exactly as this
project's ledger of 2026-08-25 says.

## ROUND THREE: no hunts at all, and three defects sixteen hunts walked past

Later the same night, on the maintainer's question of whether hunts
are the best use of a round's tokens. They were not, this time. What
ran instead was a CONSISTENCY SWEEP -- one oracle over many acts,
described in docs/process/HUNT-RECORD.md under "WHAT TO RUN INSTEAD OF
A HUNT" -- and it found three defects in ground sixteen hunts had just
finished reading, at a cost of one evening's building and about seven
minutes a run, with no verification queue at all.

WHY IT REACHED WHAT THE HUNTS DID NOT, which is the part worth
keeping. The hunts were aimed at the code written that day, and every
one of them was aimed WELL: rows 11 to 17 are all in that code. These
three are in code written on 2026-08-25 and in one case older still,
and they are reached not by an exotic journey but by the MOST ORDINARY
ONE THERE IS -- close the plugin window and open it again. A hunt
chooses where to look; a sweep enumerates what a person does. The two
find different things, and this round is the evidence.

## What the round says about hunting

EIGHT HUNTS, SEVEN WITH FINDINGS, FOUR DISTINCT DEFECTS. Three hunts
converged on defect 1 and two on defect 2, from directions chosen
before any of them had read the code — which is this method's
strongest signal and the reason the briefs name each hunt's siblings.

THE ONE THAT FOUND NOTHING EARNED ITS PLACE. It established that the
adaptive preview wait's widening is genuinely reachable from real
state (871 ms at 26 elements over a hundred columns, arming the timer
at its 350 ms ceiling; 232 ms arming it at 231), which was an open
question rather than a settled one — and it caught the false claim in
row 6 while doing it.

AND THE AIMING PAID AGAIN. Every defect here is in code written within
two days, and three are inside repairs made the same morning. Five
rounds, five times.
