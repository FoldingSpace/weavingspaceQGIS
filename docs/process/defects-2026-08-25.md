# The defects of 2026-08-25: eight hunts, eleven defects, every one in the day's own code

Written the night they were found, per the rule that a day passing
roughly ten defects gets a ledger and that the OWES column is the
record. This day did not reach ten, and the ledger exists anyway
because of the DISTRIBUTION: eight hunts in three rounds, ELEVEN
confirmed defects, and every one of them inside code written within
hours — NINE of the eleven inside repairs for defects the same day
had found, and four of those inside repairs made in the hour before.

THE CURVE DID NOT FLATTEN. Round one: four hunts, four defects. Round
two: four hunts, three defects, all created by round one's repairs.
Round three: two hunts, four defects, three of them created by round
two's repairs. A round aimed at last round's fixes has found
something every single time, which is the strongest argument this
project has yet produced for not shipping while the hunts are still
running.

THE SECOND ROUND IS THE ARGUMENT FOR THE SECOND ROUND. Four hunts
found four defects; the three hunts aimed at those four repairs found
three more, and one of them had been examined and ruled BENIGN by the
first round — it went live because a fix removed the accident that
was hiding it.

The ratio is the finding, and it matches this project's own history
exactly (2026-08-16: eleven of fourteen within hours; 2026-08-20:
four of five in that day's code). Aiming a round of hunts at fresh
work is not a precaution. It is where the defects are.

## What the shape of these eleven says

**THREE OF THE ELEVEN ARE THE SAME QUESTION ASKED IN THREE PLACES**,
and it is the night's most useful pattern: when a branch keys on the
ABSENCE of a record, ask what else can produce that absence, and what
else can BIND the thing you are keyed on. Row 2 was "the plugin
opened before the data". Row 11 was "the chooser touches our own
output during a project read". Row 1 was "a removal nulls the watched
layer". Same shape, three doors, found by three different hunts.

**A CLEANUP THAT WORKS BY SIDE EFFECT IS A CLEANUP NOBODY HAS
WRITTEN DOWN.** Row 7 is the night's best lesson. The clear list was
missing the shelf from the moment the shelf existed, and the first
round LOOKED at that omission and ruled it benign, correctly, because
something else happened to empty the record. The fix that removed the
accident was three lines long and nowhere near the clear list. So: a
list that is wrong but masked is still wrong, and the day the mask
moves is the day it costs something. When you find a record emptied
by something other than the code that owns it, write the entry
anyway.

**A FIX MADE UNDER A RULING CAN VIOLATE THE RULING BESIDE IT.** Row 5
is ruling 2 -- keep the previous dataset's map -- creating the very
loss it exists to prevent, because adoption elsewhere assumed one
group. When a ruling starts producing a SECOND artefact by default,
re-drive every path that used to assume one.

**THREE OF THE FIRST FOUR WERE INSIDE THE DAY'S OWN REPAIRS**, and the
fourth was inside the day's own feature. Defect 2 lived inside the
orphan-merge fix written a few hours earlier — a branch keyed on "no
identity yet", written for ONE route and firing for TWO. Defects 3
and 4 lived inside the whole-scheme reset. Defect 1 was in the
boundary the full suite had drawn that afternoon.

**A FIX THAT KEYS ON THE ABSENCE OF A RECORD MUST ENUMERATE EVERY
ROUTE TO THAT ABSENCE.** That is defect 2 stated generally, and it is
the most transferable line here. `old_id is None` meant "adoption
wrote these before the chooser settled". It also meant "the plugin
opened before the data", which nobody had listed.

**TWO HUNTS CONVERGING IS WORTH MORE THAN ONE HUNT AGREEING WITH
ITSELF.** Defect 1 was found from two directions with different
briefs, different probes and different second routes, and the second
hunt added what the first could not see: the layer COUNT decides
which of two code paths runs, so the same act answered differently in
a project with two polygon layers and one with three.

**A RECORD IS NOT THE THING A USER SEES.** Defect 3 was invisible to
every record-level check because `_assignments` reads widgets. The
matrix, the tests and the entries all agreed the records were right;
the map disagreed. Where a path edits the table IN PLACE, the widgets
ARE the state.

## What was ruled out, so nobody spends the time again

The banks hunt ruled out `_scheme_memory` missing from
`_forget_the_last_project` (benign: the swap-to-None empties the view
first, dump-verified) and `_quant_colours` being unbanked (an
asymmetry with no harm statable under ruling 8's value-laden
wording). The shelf hunt ruled out cross-element bleed, rows left on
"---", and one dirty oracle of its own (a second dialog adopting an
already-generated layer). The switch-acts hunt ruled out an
unofferable element count — the catalogue's extension loop fills
2..26 — and notices being swallowed.

The SECOND round ruled out more, and the list matters because these
are the places three careful hunts looked and found nothing: the
mirror risk of the reset flag (a style, ramp, count or Reverse chosen
AFTER a switch all survive it), double-arming that flag (idempotent),
door three firing on a dataset switch (it does not), the settle route
skipping `_begin_new_dataset` (measured, correct), plain project
replacement, and A-B-A and A-B-C shelf journeys.

## AND WHAT I GOT WRONG WHILE FIXING THEM, since the pattern is the
## useful part

**MY FIRST FIX FOR ROW 5 HAD A FAULT OF ITS OWN**, caught by driving
rather than by reading: the mismatch check referenced `region_source`
eighteen lines before it was assigned — an UnboundLocalError inside a
Qt slot, swallowed, which showed up only as a landing quietly reusing
the previous group. The rule this project already carries would have
caught it: assert the postcondition, not just the anchor.

**AND THE FIRST FIX FOR ROW 1 DID NOT TAKE**, because a second fault
sat under it. Correcting `switched` to ask about the dataset in force
left the route broken, since removing the layer empties the chooser
and the bank swap then banked the outgoing dataset and nulled the
identity before the next one arrived. Two faults, one route, and the
second hid the first — which is why the probe, not the reasoning, is
what confirmed each fix.
